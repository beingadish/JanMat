## Core System Architecture

This system is best conceived as a **civic deliberation + vote recording platform** — not just a ballot box, but an on-chain parliament where debate happens before decisions are made.

### Blockchain & Rust Stack

The choice of Rust is excellent for this domain. Rust's memory safety model and zero-cost abstractions make it ideal for writing smart contract runtimes and the backend API layer. You would likely build on top of a chain like **Solana** (whose entire runtime is in Rust), or write a custom substrate-based chain using **Parity Substrate** (also Rust). Smart contracts handle vote recording as immutable on-chain transactions, making tampering practically impossible. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

### Identity Layer (India-Specific)

The voter identity problem is the hardest part. India already has **Aadhaar** (1.3B+ enrollments), which could be abstracted into a **zero-knowledge proof** layer — the system verifies that a voter is a valid Indian citizen without storing or revealing their Aadhaar number on-chain. This preserves anonymity while ensuring one-person-one-vote integrity. [psa.gov](https://psa.gov.in/CMS/web/sites/default/files/psa_custom_files/Election%20Commission%20of%20India-IIT%20Madras.pdf)

### Topics, Subtopics & Discussion

The deliberation layer is what makes this more than a polling app. Structurally:

- **Topics** → high-level national issues (e.g., "Education Policy 2026")
- **Subtopics** → drill-down discussion threads (e.g., "NEP Implementation in Rural Areas")
- **Proposals** → concrete actionable resolutions that can be voted on
- **Outcomes vs. Outputs distinction**: an *output* is a policy document passed; an *outcome* is the measurable real-world change it produces — the system should track both over time on-chain as verifiable state transitions

***

## Outputs vs. Outcomes Framework

This is one of the most intellectually important design decisions. Most governance systems only record the vote (output) and never close the feedback loop on whether it caused change (outcome). Your system should include:

- **Output record**: the vote result, timestamp, block hash — immutable [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)
- **Outcome tracker**: a linked on-chain entry updated over time with verified data (e.g., government API feeds, citizen-submitted evidence) that measures the real-world impact of the decision
- **Impact score**: a derived metric that contributors can debate in the subtopic thread, creating accountability long after the vote

## What You're Actually Building

This isn't a social network. It's a **civic truth machine** — a minimal, tamper-proof, people-owned ledger of national opinion. The constraint *is* the feature. No feeds, no profiles, no clout. Just structured signal: here is an issue, here is what the country thinks, here is the proof.

***

## The Address Namespace

Your `t/`, `st/`, `o/`, `v/` schema is clean and purposeful. Here's how to formalize it:

| Prefix | Full form | Role | Mutable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `t/` | Topic | Root-level national issue (e.g., `t/education-nep-2026`) | No — locked on creation |
| `st/` | Sub-topic | Narrowing of a topic (e.g., `st/nep-rural-implementation`) | No |
| `o/` | Opinion | The authored description of *what* is being voted on. The actual statement. | No — hash-locked to chain |
| `v/` | Vote | The ballot — options (single/multi), duration, visibility toggle | Partially — visibility has one-way lock |

Every node in this tree gets a **ULID** (Universally Unique Lexicographically Sortable Identifier) — time-ordered, base32-encoded, blockchain-anchored. This is your clone-detection key: `o/01HXYZ...ABC` is the unique fingerprint, and a duplicate gets tagged `clone-of:01HXYZ...ABC` by community vote — exactly like GitHub issues. Two moderators (people-elected, term-limited) can confirm clone status, but the clone tag itself is also voted on. No moderator can unilaterally remove content — they can only surface it for a vote. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

***

## The Vote Visibility Architecture

This is arguably your most innovative feature — and cryptographically non-trivial. Here's the right way to implement it:

- **Default state**: Anonymous. Votes are recorded as **zero-knowledge proofs** — the chain proves "a valid citizen voted" without knowing *who*. The tally is public; the voter is not. [stellar](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/5-real-world-zero-knowledge-use-cases)
- **Transparency toggle (one-way, permanent)**: When the creator enables `v/reveal`, all votes — past and future — become publicly attributed. This uses a **commitment scheme**: at vote time, every voter commits `hash(identity + secret_nonce)`. If the vote is ever made public, voters reveal their nonce, and the chain verifies the commitment. The catch: **even the creator cannot see until revelation is toggled** — and once toggled, it can never be hidden again. [github](https://github.com/advaita-saha/zk-Voting)
- **Why one-way?** Because reversibility creates coercion risk. If a politician could flip visibility on/off, voters would be intimidated. Permanence is the protection.

***

## Clone Detection & Content Guard

Two separate systems working in tandem:

**Duplicate Guard (pre-submission)**
Before an `o/` opinion is published, a Rust-based semantic similarity engine runs against all existing opinions in that `t/` + `st/` namespace. If similarity exceeds a threshold, it flags it with candidate clone references and *requires the submitter to acknowledge*. The final clone verdict is still people-voted — the system only assists, never decides alone.

**Harm Filter (content moderation without censorship)**
This is a hard line: the filter bans **vectors of harm**, not topics about harm. A discussion about rape law reform is protected speech. A post that functions as a coordination tool for perpetrators is not. The rule engine works like this:
- A **blocklist** of explicit harmful coordination phrases, maintained by elected moderators + community votes to add/remove terms
- A **context classifier** (lightweight Rust-embedded ML model) that scores intent, not just keywords
- Flagged content goes to a **community review queue** — two moderators + a minimum threshold of citizen votes needed to suppress it
- All moderation decisions are **on-chain and auditable** — no shadow banning, no secret removals. Every removal has a block hash.

***

## Moderator Election Protocol

- Max **2 moderators** per `t/` topic namespace
- Elected by the same `v/` voting mechanism the platform runs on — no special process
- **Term-limited**: 90 days, renewable once by re-election
- **Zero unilateral power**: a moderator can flag, propose clone tags, and submit to the harm filter queue — but cannot delete, hide, or suppress alone
- **Recall by vote**: if 10% of voters on a topic initiate recall, a `v/` vote opens automatically

Most civic tech platforms fail because they are *designed for engagement* — which incentivizes outrage and noise. You've deliberately inverted this:

- **No likes, no shares, no follower counts** — removes all virality mechanisms

## 1. What already exists and is actually used

### Blockchain voting systems in the wild

- **Polyas** – a long‑running electronic voting provider that added blockchain elements; has been used for elections in Germany and elsewhere (unions, associations, some public bodies). [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
- **Polys (Kaspersky)** – blockchain‑based voting for universities, student councils, and professional bodies. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
- **FollowMyVote** – remote blockchain voting platform; mostly pilots and smaller elections. [globalgovernmentforum](https://www.globalgovernmentforum.com/india-to-develop-blockchain-voting-system/)
- **Agora** – ran a limited blockchain tally in the 2018 Sierra Leone presidential election, parallel to the official count; interesting pilot but not authoritative nor fully national‑scale. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
- **City of Zug (Switzerland) / Luxoft prototype** – municipal blockchain voting trials in “Crypto Valley,” focused on secure identity and transparency. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)
- **DemocracyGuard** – a newer blockchain‑based voting framework tested in pilot elections; authors report higher voter trust but emphasize regulatory and integration challenges. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)

A 2026 Indian paper also proposes and tests a **“Blockchain Based Decentralized Voting System”**; it confirms immutability and tamper‑resistance, but shows latency rising with load and positions it as a *research prototype*, not a deployed national system. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

Net: there are real deployments, but they tend to be:

- **Organizational / local**, not national
- **Operator‑controlled** (company or authority), not fully citizen‑governed
- Often **pilot / experimental**, with big unresolved issues (privacy, coercion, regulation, UX) [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

## 2. What these systems struggle with (your opportunity)

The big surveys and recent frameworks converge on the same pain points: [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

1. **Privacy vs verifiability vs coercion‑resistance**
   - Getting anonymity, end‑to‑end verifiability, and resistance to vote‑selling/coercion *all at once* is still unsolved; no protocol fully satisfies every ideal voting property. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
   - Many systems either trust a set of authorities for decryption, or weaken receipt‑freeness, or sacrifice usability.

2. **Scalability and performance**
   - Transaction speed and throughput are repeatedly cited as primary blockers to national‑scale adoption. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)
   - Prototypes show good behavior at tens of thousands of votes but rising latency as voters scale into the hundreds of thousands and above. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

3. **Centralization of power**
   - Even “decentralized” voting systems often rely on **centralized identity providers, trusted authorities, or platform companies** to operate the system. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)
   - Governance of the rules (who can create polls, how results are interpreted) usually sits outside the chain.

4. **Usability and public trust**
   - Non‑technical users find blockchain voting **confusing and opaque**; user testing in recent frameworks shows high trust among technical users, but hesitation among general citizens. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)
   - Legal and regulatory uncertainty also blocks broad deployment; people and institutions don’t know if results will be recognized in law. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)

5. **Coercion and vote‑buying**
   - Remote voting from uncontrolled environments amplifies the risk of vote‑buying and coercion, and existing systems mostly don’t have a satisfying solution beyond “allow re‑voting and hope it helps.” [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)

Your concept—minimal, no‑feed interface; strong rules about clones; citizen‑chosen moderators; optional public vote visibility—is already pushing in directions these systems usually don’t.

## 3. What you can add to make it *more* democratized

Here are concrete, research‑backed additions that move you beyond today’s systems and make the whole thing more “people‑owned.”

### 3.1 Hard‑coded voting properties from the literature

The Jafar survey and others define a set of **security/legitimacy properties**: eligibility, unreusability (one-person-one-vote), privacy, fairness, soundness, completeness, verifiability, coercion‑resistance, etc. Make these first‑class constraints of your protocol, not afterthoughts: [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)

- **Eligibility & singularity**: one vote per verified citizen, enforced via zkKYC + one credential per person. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)
- **Universal + individual verifiability**:  
  - Every citizen can verify their own vote is recorded and tallied correctly.  
  - Anyone can audit that the totals match the recorded (encrypted) ballots. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)
- **Fairness**: no intermediate results; all tallies are decrypted / revealed only at the close time. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
- **Robustness**: protocol must tolerate some percentage of malicious nodes without breaking the election. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)

Design choice: literally **encode these as invariants** in Rust smart contracts, with formal tests, and make failure to satisfy them impossible at the protocol level.

### 3.2 Stronger coercion‑resistance

The literature notes coercion and vote‑buying as hard unsolved challenges in online voting, especially remote voting. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)

You can do better by:

- Allowing **re‑voting until the poll closes**; only the last valid ballot counts (this weakens receipts). [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
- Disallowing any cryptographic receipt that can prove to a third party how someone voted (receipt‑freeness). [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
- Optionally providing **“deniable casts”**: voters can generate fake proofs to show a coercer, while their real vote remains private.
- Making your **“public visibility” toggle only available for non‑sensitive decisions**, and clearly warning that it destroys coercion‑resistance when enabled.

### 3.3 zk‑based identity and ticketing

Zero‑knowledge frameworks suggest a **zkKYC + zkVoting** approach: [blockchain.gov](https://blockchain.gov.in/Home/CaseStudy?CaseStudy=remotevotingchain)

- Citizens verify once with a trusted authority (e.g., Aadhaar‑backed KYC provider in India) and receive a **ZK credential** saying “Indian citizen, eligible voter,” stored client‑side. [globalgovernmentforum](https://www.globalgovernmentforum.com/india-to-develop-blockchain-voting-system/)
- To vote, they prove in zero‑knowledge:
  - “I hold a valid credential”
  - “I have not yet voted on this v/”
- The chain verifies the proof and counts their encrypted vote, but never sees Aadhaar or identity data. [blockchain.gov](https://blockchain.gov.in/Home/CaseStudy?CaseStudy=remotevotingchain)

This matches your “no hack, no control, full power to people” ethos: no central authority can see who voted what, but everyone can verify the rules were enforced.

### 3.4 Constitutional / meta‑governance layer

Most systems treat protocol rules as out‑of‑band. A lot of research on citizen participation stresses **institutional trust and clear rules** as key to legitimacy. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)

You can add:

- A **“constitution contract”**:  
  - Defines how rules change (quorums, thresholds, supermajorities).  
  - Governs moderator powers, clone rules, word bans, etc.
- Any change to these rules must go through its own v/ vote (with stricter requirements), so **even the protocol is subject to democracy**.
- Allow **citizen‑initiated referenda**: any t/ topic that reaches N supporting signatures automatically triggers a binding v/.

### 3.5 Inclusion and accessibility

Frameworks emphasise that availability, accessibility, and usability are core voting requirements, not nice‑to‑haves. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

Concrete additions:

- **Low‑bandwidth, low‑end‑device UX** and an SMS/IVR or kiosk‑based flow so people without smartphones or reliable internet aren’t excluded.
- Full **multi‑language support** (all major Indian languages), with the *ballot text* being the only thing users see — no crypto jargon. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)
- Accessibility features: screen‑reader friendly UI, high‑contrast modes, etc.

This is where you can radically outperform most blockchain voting pilots, which are often tech‑centric and small‑scale. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

### 3.6 Transparent, citizen‑controlled moderation

Most proposals rely on administrators or platform operators for moderation. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)

You’re already ahead with citizen‑elected moderators; you can strengthen it by:

- Making all **moderation actions on‑chain**: every removal, clone tagging, word‑ban proposal is a transaction with a reason code. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)
- Adding **appeal flows**:
  - A moderator action can be escalated to a random citizen jury (sampled from verified citizens) to approve or overturn.
  - This is aligned with research exploring citizen participation architectures and blockchain‑based accountability. [papers.ssrn](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5770662)
- Having **hard limits** on moderator powers (e.g., they can only flag + route to vote, not delete directly).

### 3.7 Hybrid chain architecture for scale

Recent frameworks argue for **hybrid architectures** (permissioned + permissionless) to balance scalability and decentralization. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)

For your Rust/Web3 stack:

- Use a **permissioned, high‑throughput chain** (possibly built on Substrate or similar) for raw vote transactions.
- Periodically anchor Merkle roots of vote batches onto a **public chain** (e.g., a Rust‑friendly L1/L2) for global transparency and immutability. [github](https://github.com/topics/blockchain-voting-system)
- Combine this with **zk rollups** or zkVMs (e.g., RISC Zero) so heavy verification happens off‑chain and only succinct proofs go on‑chain. [blockchain.gov](https://blockchain.gov.in/Home/CaseStudy?CaseStudy=remotevotingchain)

This directly addresses the transaction speed and scalability issues identified in surveys. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)

Based on current research and deployments, here’s how your design can outperform:

1. **Full-stack democracy, not just “online ballots”**

4. **Explicit handling of harmful content without killing hard topics**

   Your separation — allow the former, prevent the latter via word‑ban lists, intent classification, and citizen‑audited moderation — is exactly the nuance missing in most technical papers.

5. **End‑to‑end audited moderation and rule changes**  
   Research stresses transparency and auditability as key to trust, not just for votes but for the whole system. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
   By logging **every** rule change, ban, clone‑tag, and moderator action on‑chain and making them queryable, you offer a level of *governance transparency* that even many blockchain systems don’t.

6. **India‑first, Aadhaar‑aware, zk‑respecting**  
   Most deployed systems are designed in Western contexts with different ID systems and legal frameworks. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8434614/)
   Building an India‑centric system that:
   - Integrates with Aadhaar‑grade identity (through zkKYC, not raw IDs), and  
   - Aligns with India’s National Blockchain Framework trajectory,  
   makes it far more realistic to be noticed by government and media as a legitimate “public pulse” instrument.
